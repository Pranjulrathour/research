# The AGI Transition: A Field Guide for the Next Decade
### Pranjul Rathour

**Promise to the reader.** Not predictions. A set of frameworks that let you reason about any AI development between now and
2036, and a plan for staying valuable while the ground moves. Written from India, for people who will work through this
transition rather than watch it.

**Design rules.** Every chapter ends with "What would make this chapter wrong" and "What to do this year". No chapter depends on a
product that could vanish. Every number has a source and a year attached, so a reader in 2031 can see what was known when.
Durable spine: capability frameworks, economics of automation, evaluation, governance, institutions, personal strategy.

**Target length.** ~45,000 words; 12 chapters of 3,000-4,200 words; front matter; a sources list per chapter.

---

## Part I — Where we are and how to tell

### 1. What "general" means, and why the word matters
- Narrow vs general: the capability-breadth × autonomy framing (DeepMind's *Levels of AGI*, Morris et al. 2023/2024) as a map, not a prophecy
- Why "is it AGI yet?" is the wrong question; "which level, on which tasks, at what reliability" is the right one
- The three recurring confusions: benchmark performance vs job performance; fluency vs competence; demos vs deployments
- *What would make this wrong*: a capability jump that collapses the levels. *Do this year*: learn to read an evaluation.

### 2. The measurement problem
- Why benchmarks saturate (training on the test, Goodhart, ceiling effects) and what survives: held-out, contamination-resistant, task-based evaluation
- Reading a model card and a technical report: what is disclosed, what is estimated, what is missing
- Evaluation as a civic skill: journalists, managers and voters will all be asked to judge capability claims
- *Do this year*: run one public benchmark yourself and see where it breaks.

### 3. The economics: four effects, not one
- Acemoglu-Restrepo style decomposition: displacement, augmentation (productivity), new tasks, and demand effects; why history (ATMs, spreadsheets, containers) shows all four at once
- Tasks, not jobs: the task-exposure literature (Eloundou et al., ILO/IMF exposure indices) and its limits
- Why wages, not headlines, are the thing to watch
- *Do this year*: decompose your own job into tasks; mark each as exposed / augmented / new.

## Part II — Institutions under pressure

### 4. Work: the shape of the next decade
- Scenarios, not forecasts: slow diffusion, uneven diffusion, fast diffusion; what each implies for entry-level roles, which is where students live
- The entry-level paradox: the tasks that train juniors are the easiest to automate; how firms respond (apprenticeship redesign, fewer but broader hires)
- India specifically: services exports, the IT-services model, GCCs, and what "value migration" means for a graduate in 2030
- *Do this year*: pick the skill that is hardest to automate in your target role and get measurably better at it.

### 5. Learning: schools, colleges and the end of the essay as proof
- Assessment collapse and what replaces it: oral exams, portfolios, supervised work, process evidence
- The tutor effect (Bloom's two-sigma problem revisited) and its real-world ceiling: access, motivation, and the gap between a tutor and a syllabus
- What an MCA/B.Tech curriculum should look like when the compiler explains itself
- *Do this year*: build one thing a model cannot do for you and document how you did it.

### 6. Science and engineering: faster, not automatically better
- Where AI accelerates research (literature, code, simulation, protein and materials search) and where the bottleneck moves (experiments, verification, review)
- The reproducibility dividend: why code-plus-data papers become the standard of credibility
- *Do this year*: reproduce one published result end to end.

### 7. Governance: who decides, and how
- The patterns that recur across jurisdictions: risk tiers, transparency and disclosure duties, evaluation and incident reporting, compute thresholds, liability
- EU AI Act, US executive actions and state laws, China's rules, India's approach (IndiaAI Mission, advisories, DPDP Act) compared on those dimensions, with dates
- The institutions being built: safety institutes, standards bodies, audit ecosystems
- *Do this year*: read one law's actual text, not a summary.

### 8. Trust, security and the information commons
- Synthetic media, provenance (C2PA), authentication by default; why "default to verification" becomes a norm
- Prompt injection, agent permissions and the new attack surface of systems that act
- Public knowledge after the web is mostly machine-written: the incentives for original, verified work rise, not fall
- *Do this year*: adopt provenance and verification habits in your own publishing.

## Part III — Living through it

### 9. Agents: when software starts acting
- From answering to acting: tool use, planning loops, permissions, oversight; why reliability, not intelligence, gates deployment
- The economics of delegation: when a 95%-reliable agent is worth it and when it is worthless
- *Do this year*: run an agent on a real task with a budget and a kill switch; write down where it failed.

### 10. The personal skill portfolio for 2026-2036
- Three durable layers: judgment (problem framing, verification, taste), systems (how things fit and fail), and domain depth
- Why "AI skills" is the wrong frame; "skills that compound with AI" is the right one
- A one-page plan: what to learn, what to stop learning, what to build in public, how to prove it
- *Do this year*: publish one piece of verifiable work per quarter.

### 11. India's decade
- Demographics, digital public infrastructure (Aadhaar, UPI, ONDC) as a platform for AI diffusion, language diversity as both barrier and moat
- The GCC wave and the risk of being the world's AI back office vs building for a billion users
- Education at scale: what AI tutoring can and cannot fix in Indian classrooms
- *Do this year*: build something for an Indian language or an Indian institution.

### 12. How to be wrong well
- Keeping a decision journal through the transition; updating on evidence, not vibes
- Base rates from previous general-purpose technologies (electricity, computing, the internet): decades of diffusion, uneven returns, institutional lag
- What this book expects to get wrong, listed plainly
- A closing checklist: the ten habits of someone who comes out of this decade stronger
